All Members · Strategic Wrap-Up

Course Synthesis & Strategic Q&A Wrap-Up

Plain-language, crystal-clear answers to the 6 core clinical and machine learning questions.

⏱️ Q&A Timer (10 Mins)
10:00
Khương & Hiếu Question 1

❓ Why not just use a standard risk prediction model instead of causal inference?

Plain Answer: A risk model only answers "Who is sick?" (correlation). It cannot answer "Who will get better if we give them the medicine?" (causality). Prescribing based only on high risk wastes costly drugs and causes avoidable side effects.

☂️ Simple Analogy: Predicting who carries an umbrella is risk prediction. Knowing who stays dry because they opened the umbrella is causal inference!
Hiếu Question 2

❓ Is a C-for-benefit score of 0.60–0.65 good enough for clinical practice?

Plain Answer: Yes, absolutely! In treatment effect ML, we never observe counterfactuals for the same patient, so we compare statistical twin pairs.

🏆 Hospital Benchmark: Because twin matching adds unavoidable statistical noise, 0.60–0.65 is the recognized international gold standard (equivalent to a strong 0.80 AUC in standard risk prediction).
Hiếu Question 3

❓ Which model is better: S-Learner or T-Learner?

Plain Answer: T-Learner is significantly better for medicine. The S-Learner puts the drug treatment feature into one single tree with dozens of patient features (like Age and SBP); the tree ignores the drug, shrinking estimated benefit to zero (*Shrinkage Bias*).

🌲🌲 Why T-Learner Wins: T-Learner trains 2 separate trees (Treated vs Control), capturing 100% of treatment interactions without bias.
Linh Question 4

❓ Does a Grad-CAM heatmap prove that the AI model is medically correct?

Plain Answer: No! Grad-CAM shows WHERE the model looked, NOT that its medical reasoning was right.

🔍 Clinical Safety Check: If the heatmap highlights a hospital metal marker or bone instead of the lung lesion, doctors catch the AI cheating before it makes a diagnosis!
Linh & Dung Question 5

❓ If rule-based NLP is dated compared to GPT-4, why do we still study it?

Plain Answer: Real hospital PACS systems have millions of unlabelled notes. Cloud LLMs are expensive, slow, and violate strict patient privacy laws (HIPAA).

⚡ On-Premise Power: Regex and medical ontologies (UMLS/NegBio) run 100% locally inside hospital firewalls at near-zero cost with deterministic accuracy.
Dung Question 6

❓ Labels extracted by rules are noisy — why does training a CNN on them still work?

Plain Answer: Deep Learning CNNs act as natural noise filters at scale.

🤖 Big Data Magic: Across 100,000+ scans, random rule labeling errors cancel out. The DenseNet121 CNN learns true visual features that generalize far better than the noisy rules!
